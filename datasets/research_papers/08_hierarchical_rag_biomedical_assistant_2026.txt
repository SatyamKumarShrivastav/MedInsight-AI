Title: Hierarchical RAG Enhances a Pharmacogenomic AI Assistant in Guideline Related Queries
Authors: Jeon, Y., et al.
Journal: Computers in Biology and Medicine
Year: 2026
DOI: 10.1016/j.compbiomed.2026.109842 (PubMed: 41319469)
Category: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), Natural Language Processing, Biomedical Assistant

ABSTRACT:
Standard naive retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) models often experience significant context fragmentation when querying dense, multi-tiered biomedical guidelines, frequently returning disjointed text snippets that omit crucial clinical contraindications. In this study, we introduce a Hierarchical Retrieval-Augmented Generation (HRAG) architecture tailored for complex clinical and pharmacogenomic queries. HRAG indexes medical literature across a multi-level hierarchy (document metadata, section summaries, and fine-grained text chunks), drastically reducing hallucination rates and improving citation accuracy.

METHODOLOGY:
1. Document Parsing & Hierarchical Chunking: Ingesting Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium (CPIC) and FDA guidance documents. Parsing extracts structural trees (Title -> Section Header -> Paragraph -> Sub-bullet).
2. Two-Stage Retrieval:
   - Coarse-Grained Routing: Dense embedding similarity identifies the top-level relevant chapters and sections.
   - Fine-Grained Evidence Selection: BM25 / TF-IDF sparse lexical scoring combined with semantic vector reranking isolates exact sentences containing dosing recommendations and metabolizer phenotypes.
3. Grounded Synthesis & Citation Tracing: The language model is constrained via strict system prompting to synthesize answers exclusively from retrieved context chunks, annotating every generated assertion with an exact chunk provenance identifier.
4. Evaluation: Benchmarked using the RAGAS framework (faithfulness, answer relevance, context recall) and evaluated by 10 clinical pharmacologists across 350 challenging clinical test queries.

RESULTS:
HRAG demonstrated a 94.6% faithfulness score on RAGAS (compared to 71.2% for naive flat RAG) and reduced factual hallucination rates to less than 1.8%. The structured citation tracing enabled clinicians to verify evidence in under 15 seconds per query.
